# THE MMOFPS architecture

## Runtime now

The client uses Three.js/WebGL2 and an adaptive rendering profile. The CPU generates nearby terrain in workers from the same deterministic implicit world used by the server's collision queries. Coarse terrain covers travel while fine chunks stream. Original movement and physics remain shared source, not a visually similar replacement controller.

Terrain detail blends three world-space projections and one macro sample from a generated noise texture. Surface-gradient normals and roughness change the shading without displacing terrain or changing collision. Industrial materials use object/instance scale coordinates so panels stay attached to moving vehicles. Rocks use physical lighting; authored cylinder and canopy normals remain smooth. The sky uses two cloud-texture samples in one dome pass; the reflection bake shares immutable generated cloud pixels but owns its GPU texture. Nearby sun shadows remain desktop-only, and mobile retains its smaller visibility and pixel-ratio budgets.

Native vegetation uses curved fanwood membranes and layered cold-climate fronds at the original deterministic tree placements. Indexed near geometry is bounded to 224 triangles per membrane cluster or 144 per frond whorl; distant crowns retain open silhouettes in 72/60 triangles instead of opaque balls/cones. The renderer retains its fixed instance limits and collider-sized stems. Two generated 128 px textures add tissue veins without network assets or alpha cutout overdraw. Physical materials supply roughness and filtered surface-normal detail. Nearby plants participate in the bounded desktop sun map; their depth material shares the visible shader's wind expression. Mobile/low quality retain the cheaper lighting path. Blue and green foliage both follow the city vegetation palettes. These are procedural art improvements, not a completed photorealistic environment.

On devices with renderable floating-point textures, `ContactLighting` renders the world once into an HDR color/depth/stencil target. It preserves supported multisampling up to four samples, then reconstructs nearby surface normals from depth. Contact shading uses half resolution with 12 desktop or 8 touch samples, fades out between 90 and 180 metres, and receives a depth-aware nine-tap filter during the final ACES/sRGB output pass. Pixel-centre reconstruction and nonzero edge derivatives prevent flat-surface bands. Cave portals retain their stencil behavior; cave fog adapts to the target's output color space. Underwater views suppress contact darkening. Low quality or missing float-target support retains direct rendering. This costs two fullscreen passes and additional GPU memory, and does not establish physical-phone performance.

Weapon metal and composite finishes use generated mipmapped grain in each assembly's coordinates. The pulse rifle adds rounded receiver edges, separate casing plates, an open cooling frame, induction rings and a circular sight. First-person geometry is 2,520 triangles in seven draws, including the independently animated magazine and bolt. The shared distant model is 1,074 triangles in three draws. Muzzle position, grip contacts, weapon rules and reload choreography are preserved.

Orbital boarding gantries and illuminated decks reuse the existing instanced transport groups. Their structural collision boxes are part of the canonical shared transport plan; the versioned protocol prevents an older client from predicting against a different plan. Routes, schedules, vehicle handling, original movement and terrain generation are preserved. These shading and terminal changes do not establish photorealistic art or physical-phone performance.

Each planet has its own Node.js process. The gateway serves the client and forwards binary WebSocket traffic to the character's saved planet. A private HTTP coordinator owns character location, inventory and session epochs in SQLite WAL with synchronous FULL commits. The default launcher starts the gateway/coordinator and all three workers locally; `LOCAL_PLANETS` and private endpoint settings allow moving a worker to another machine.

At 60 Hz each worker consumes bounded input queues, simulates movement, vehicles, projectiles and combat, records lag-compensation history, and sends binary snapshots at 30 Hz. At 5 Hz the frontier layer updates territory and discovery state; it sends a separate strategic message at 1 Hz. The legacy protocol's 60-character name/chat string field is not used for strategic messages.

Replication rebuilds player and projectile spatial buckets once per broadcast, then visits cells around each subscriber. Exact 3D distance checks and the original entity caps still apply. Owned distant siege shells are separately retained. This changes remote pose candidate selection from a scan of the whole population per subscriber to a scan of nearby buckets. Combat, AI, history recording, score/roster broadcasting and some world systems still have whole-node loops. Dense fights are capped in visibility; no claim is made that all players in an arbitrarily dense battle can be shown or simulated together.

Independent affiliation is the default. Damage and blast impulse policy protect independent players and teammates. Opposing faction AI seeks other combatants and capture objectives. Human capture weight is 1 and bot weight is 0.2; simultaneous opposing presence contests a district. Joining a vehicle crew requires compatible allegiance. Environmental hazards and self damage remain active.

Workers register every 2 seconds and checkpoint active characters in the same heartbeat cycle. Node registrations expire after 8 seconds; character leases expire after 12 seconds. Workers use conservative monotonic deadlines derived from request start times and check them before every simulation tick. Simulation pauses when node authority cannot be renewed. Registrations and world epochs now persist in SQLite. Checkpoints/releases carry a character holder and epoch and must also match that character's saved world incarnation, preventing delayed writes from a retired world even before its character lease expires. A crash can lose play since the last acknowledged checkpoint, ordinarily about 2 seconds plus scheduling/network delay.

Travel charges at an actual transit pod and cancels on movement, injury, death or vehicle entry. On departure the worker freezes/removes the character before requesting a transaction that saves cargo, changes planet, advances the epoch and reserves a destination slot for 30 seconds. Transfer IDs are idempotent. A lost response causes an ownership query before rollback; otherwise the client reconnects and the gateway resolves its saved planet. A destination that goes offline after commit keeps the character there until that world recovers.

`WorldStore` still saves each planet's territory state every 30 seconds with serialized writes, temporary-file fsync and rename. These world files are not replicated by the coordinator. Standalone `MMOFPS_ROLE=single` also uses the earlier per-character files. Existing Meridian characters are imported when absent from the universe database; legacy discovery keys are qualified by planet seed. Corrupt records produce visible errors instead of silently wiping progress.

The gateway supplies a high-entropy HttpOnly identity cookie before the automatic WebSocket connection. Workers accept authenticated private connections and a forwarded hashed identity. The browser never needs to enter a callsign or account. Duplicate tabs close the older session with code 4001, which stops automatic retries. Transient interruptions use bounded reconnect backoff. Cookie identity is local to the browser; account recovery/linking remains future work.

The supervisor restarts a crashed local worker after its registration expires. A new process can recover characters after the previous world is fenced; live same-world character leases still prevent duplicate sessions, and committed arrival reservations still count against capacity. Other planets keep running. The coordinator remains a single writer without high availability. A coordinator outage pauses worlds after their authority deadlines. Recovery conservatively fences replacement authorities for a full eight seconds, regardless of host clock corrections; orderly worker shutdown explicitly releases its world lease. Gateway buffers and checkpoint batches are bounded. Only one live authority may register per configured `(planet, region)`. The current launcher still starts one `world` region per planet.

Protocol 23 encodes entity references as canonical base-128 integers with up to 53 safe bits (1–8 bytes). Persistent human IDs remain in the low 31-bit range; zero and the legacy all-ones sentinel remain reserved. Coordinated workers obtain never-reissued blocks above the legacy 32-bit space before constructing Game. A durable SQLite ledger stores one row per batch of 65,536-ID blocks, with a monotonically increasing global sequence. The initial eight-block buffer is replenished asynchronously when two blocks remain. Game and Wildlife consume one synchronous pool across temporary entity kinds, without a per-entity coordinator call. Gaps belong to other workers and are skipped; exhausted buffers fail instead of wrapping. Legacy local namespace allocation remains for standalone mode and compatibility tests.

Bootstrap issuance binds a fresh process ID to one planet/region; an exact response-loss retry is allowed only before registration. Refills require a live authority epoch and stable request ID, including cached replies. A cold replacement receives new blocks under its fresh process UUID, so a hull or projectile still alive elsewhere cannot collide with replacement spawns. The regional handoff protocol checks initial hull provenance against this issuance ledger, then follows committed ownership. IDs are never reclaimed, including unused startup blocks. The final safe-integer bound is checked explicitly. Worker health exposes remaining IDs and prefetch/failure state. These checks remove an identity-lifetime hazard; they do not establish player capacity.

Each planet has a persistent 60 Hz clock. A registration transaction reserves time through the issued authority's entire lease before replying. The coordinator uses monotonic elapsed time while running. On restart it advances beyond the saved reservation horizon, preventing time rewind even if the host wall clock moved backward. Workers initialize transport time before admitting actors, then synchronize from heartbeat round trips (with measured RTT/2 uncertainty). This is a shared time domain, not a claim of exact phase synchronization across arbitrary networks.

Universe scheduling executes every owed tick, yielding after four steps or eight milliseconds. It retains debt during short stalls. More than five seconds of debt closes the worker, flushes whatever character/world state it can still authoritatively save, and lets the supervisor start a cold replacement; it never jumps live actors across missing physics. Coordinator recovery can deliberately cause this restart and a short reconnect interruption. Transient projectiles and unsaved play are not recovered by this crash path. Standalone legacy mode retains its old scheduler. Welcome, input render time, snapshot time, airlift start time and pong ticks use checked 64-bit numbers, avoiding the previous 32-bit wrap after about 2.27 years. Input sequences retain unsigned 32-bit wrap handling; entity references use the independent safe-integer format above. Worker health includes authority epoch/readiness, target tick, clock uncertainty and debt.

Profiling with the strict scheduler exposed costly Cinder bot visibility bursts. Bots now retain a visible opponent, rotate occluded candidates with at most two traces per scan, stagger scans across actors, and sense at 3.3 Hz while searching / 6.7 Hz with a visible target. Input and movement still run at 60 Hz. Static box collision uses conservative signed axis bounds to prune hierarchy branches; leaf pieces use the unchanged exact distance primitive. These changes improve the local default population, not distributed player capacity.

Fully graded ordinary cities and Aurelia also have an exact near-surface terrain path. It bypasses natural noise/road generation only within the authored district boundary and outside cave approaches and station grading. Deep rock and the Vesper abyss retain canonical terrain columns; all props, cuts, camps and moving transport still contribute to each query. The shared movement and physics files are unchanged. Paired local 15-bot fixtures reduced mean tick time by 28–44% across the three planets with identical checked state; [profiling details and limits](docs/PERFORMANCE.md) distinguish this from distributed capacity.

The simulation now captures and imports a complete actor group, expanding vehicle crew dependencies before any deletion. Records include mutable player state, queues, weapon timers, inventory, hull motion, owned projectiles and recent player hit history. Imports validate the entire record and reject conflicting IDs, missing connections, incomplete crews and incompatible world clocks before installing actors. Successful same-planet imports preserve momentum and queue Join events without a respawn event. Local rollback retains the original connection objects and pauses actor timers for the interruption. Failed planetary launches use this rollback path; successful planetary arrival still uses a saved profile at the destination terminal. This is preparation for regional ownership, not an enabled regional cluster. See [docs/REGIONAL-HANDOFF.md](docs/REGIONAL-HANDOFF.md).

## Geographic ownership protocol

The coordinator has durable, immutable region layouts, independent authority leases, coordinate-based reconnect routing and indexed destination capacity reservations. A layout covers the existing planar surface with ordered X/Z cuts. Regions may be unavailable independently; fresh/orbital arrivals use the configured entry region. Layout maintenance requires expired planetary world and character leases and advances saved ownership epochs. Existing whole-planet SQLite authorities migrate without resetting their fencing history. Workers cannot register bounds that are absent from the installed layout.

A staged regional transfer persists a complete runtime record and reserves every human and carried hull. Source checkpoint/release/camp traffic is locked while it is unresolved. The destination must acknowledge the staged hash and compatible time after validating its simulation; reading the record alone grants no authority. One SQLite transaction then moves every crew member and hull, assigning new character epochs and destination holders. Retries return the same decision and fixed lease times, and a late abort cannot undo commit. Coordinator restart aborts preparations, retains committed outcomes and rejects runtime import beyond the clock window. Full payloads are pruned after one minute and smaller outcomes after one day during subsequent preparations.

Tests exercise this private HTTP protocol using two Game simulations, including actual movement across a seam, a deliberately lost successful commit response, aircraft return journeys, capacity pressure and restart recovery. The launcher/WorldNode/gateway do not yet orchestrate seamless crossings. Bot controllers, foreign entity-ID lifetimes, static spawn/respawn ownership, region-scoped camps and neighboring combat still require integration. The SQLite coordinator remains a single writer and has not been qualified at high population. Details and activation prerequisites are in [docs/REGIONAL-HANDOFF.md](docs/REGIONAL-HANDOFF.md).

## Persistent field camps

The default universe runtime now owns camp construction. Its SQLite transaction writes a module, a cumulative salvage-spending receipt and the character checkpoint together. Requests use an idempotency key. Checkpoints, releases and planetary transfers reconcile their saved spending watermark with the ledger, so an older in-flight snapshot cannot refund an already committed module. Both the character epoch and current registered world authority must match. A lost response followed by a fenced retry is still an unknown outcome: the worker pauses simulation and reloads its persistent collider set before resuming.

Modules use stable database IDs in a separate entity-kind map. Records store position, quarter-turn orientation, builder and allegiance; they survive character movement and planet travel. Only the owner can pack one, without a refund. The coordinator enforces six owned modules across planets, four per 128 m cell and a current 8,192-module budget per planet. Startup and heartbeat load the authority's geographic rectangle plus a 384 m neighbor margin; unchanged views return only a cursor. Mutation requires the character and module location to share the current geographic authority. The live launcher still uses one authority per planet and a single SQLite writer.

Each construction transaction returns the complete current 128 m cell and its durable revision, including an empty set after its final module is packed. The regional replica preserves newer committed cells when older full snapshots arrive, without blocking freshness acknowledgements during continuous building. An idempotent operation retry retains its original spending receipt but can return a newer cell view. Unchanged module identities and collision-cell stamps are preserved. Workers refresh every two seconds and pause when their camp view is about seven seconds old, even if their authority lease still renews. Revision counters and removal metadata survive restart; legacy positions are backfilled transactionally in bounded pages. This is asynchronous neighbor visibility, not synchronized cross-region collision activation. Construction routing across seams and full regional worker orchestration remain incomplete; see [docs/REGIONAL-HANDOFF.md](docs/REGIONAL-HANDOFF.md).

The shared world field unions camp boxes and clipped ramp volumes into movement and projectile collision. Terrain workers have an empty dynamic field and do not bake these structures into chunks. The renderer instances three procedural models and their lights, with at most six ordinary camp draws plus a placement ghost. A cached collision neighborhood avoids repeated spatial lookups for adjacent capsule probes; mutations invalidate it. Movement/physics constants and algorithms are preserved.

Each client subscribes to a 5 by 5 neighborhood of construction cells, at most 100 modules (2,303 bytes). Per-cell versions suppress unchanged messages and distant edits. Committed construction is sent immediately to affected players; ordinary cell transitions update at 1 Hz. At 1 Hz, service modules check allegiance, proximity, line of sight and combat cooldown. Medical recovery does not stack; fabricators replenish finite ordinary reserves and exclude spawn-empty/infinite-reserve weapons.

Placement checks dry, supported surface terrain, slope, city/road/transit exclusion, existing modules, players, vehicles, range, line of sight, costs, density and concurrent construction reservations. Modules are currently indestructible; there are no resource deposits, decay, construction upgrades, ownership transfer, respawn anchors or distant personal-camp map markers. The legacy standalone server does not expose construction. These remain explicit gameplay/integration limits.

## Path to the requested scale

The full objective is hundreds of thousands of simultaneous participants distributed through one persistent universe, with optional faction war and meaningful exploration. It needs several explicit ownership boundaries:

1. An edge/gateway fleet accepts browsers and routes them by a versioned universe/region directory.
2. Authoritative region workers own bounded geographic areas or planet regions. A planet cannot remain the only scaling unit once one planet is popular.
3. The implemented durable player/transfer service needs partitioning and availability work before it can serve the requested population. Its fencing, reservation and idempotency semantics must survive that change.
4. Neighbor workers exchange boundary interest and resolve vehicles, passengers, projectiles and combat consistently through handoff. Spherical coordinates/local frames must eventually replace assumptions about a single global up direction.
5. Strategic territory, structures, resources, squads and logistics use durable shared storage and event streams at lower frequency than combat. Unobserved AI runs at a cheaper strategic level and materializes consistently when players arrive.
6. Regional interest, animation/LOD and network budgets must be validated under distributed populations and dense hot spots. Load balancing must consider simulation time, bandwidth and travel, not just connection count.

The intended home server can run several services/processes during development. Aggregate traffic and simulation capacity at large scale require additional machines and measured network capacity. The old and current small local checks do not certify that infrastructure.

## Acceptance evidence still needed

Preserve a continuous playable route through a city, optional local fighting, railway/wilderness, a cave network, coast/ferry, a second city, multiple vehicle classes, construction/logistics and a launch to a geographically different planet. Exercise the same route with predicted motion and authoritative state, not a free-camera recording.

Measure first playable frame, payload/download budgets, physical-phone frame times, server tick debt, p95/p99 snapshot gaps, transfer interruption recovery, storage restart/recovery, long-flight streaming and player churn. Large load tests must include actual subscriptions/serialization and realistic input, and separate universe-wide concurrency from dense-battle concurrency.
